# Bach Listening Guide

*Cantabile & Harmony · Eleven Hearing Stages*

> Cantabile is the way we come to hear Truth in Bach.

# The vision

The purpose of this guide is not to collect beautiful performances, but to cultivate truthful hearing. Truth means the relationships from which Bach’s expression arises: bass, harmony, voice-leading, rhythm, phrase, counterpoint, proportion, form, and transformation. Cantabile—making the musical line sing—is our way into those relationships. Singing is not an imposed sentiment or an instruction to make every note lyrical in the same way. Every voice must be capable of singing, even when one voice rightly stands in the foreground.

The chosen recordings are listening lenses, not authorities. They are selected for how clearly they reveal musical causes. Tone, touch, articulation, tempo, and instrumental color are structural means, not decorative ends. Beauty emerges when the relationships are not obscured. Listen first for why the music moves, then for how it moves you.

# How to use the guide

- Follow the stages in order, but do not rush. Some stages contain complete cycles and large vocal works; they invite return visits.
- Listen first without interruption. On a second hearing, follow the score and, for vocal works, a translation. Track the bass, cadences, entrances, meter and large-scale returns.
- Sing one line, even imperfectly. At the keyboard, play a different line while singing the first. Listen for the harmony and rhythmic necessity connecting them.
- Use each stage’s question as an inquiry, not an exam. Write down one concrete event you heard: a bass turn, suspension, entrance, cadence, changed return or formal proportion.
- Compare performances only after identifying what the composition asks to be heard. The curated artists provide a starting point, not a final verdict.
- For each stage, complete the listening experiment before moving on. The goal is a more discriminating ear, not the completion of a checklist.
# The eleven stages

## 01  Ground

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of harmonic relationships.

**Listening question** — What does the bass know that the melody has not yet told us?

**Direction** — Find the tonal center, sing the bass, and notice how each harmony creates an expectation.

**Recording itinerary** — Trifonov: Petzold Minuet BWV Anh.114 and Bach BWV511; Hewitt: selected Little Preludes BWV924–943 and BWV999. The Petzold Minuet is not by J. S. Bach.

**At the piano** — At the piano, sing the bass while playing the upper voice; then reverse. Mark a cadence and explain why it feels conclusive.

## 02  Breath

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical becoming.

**Listening question** — What makes this phrase need to continue—and what finally allows it to rest?

**Direction** — Listen to sung phrases before their instrumental reflections. Attend to inhalation, suspension, release, and cadence.

**Recording itinerary** — BWV177/V→BWV639; BWV208 vocal aria→Petri/Fleisher transcription; BWV12 Sinfonia→chorus→Ólafsson; finish with only the opening BWV82 aria sung by Lorraine Hunt Lieberson.

**At the piano** — Sing a phrase without words; locate the harmony that asks it to continue, and the cadence that lets it rest.

**Playlist tracks** — 21–35 · 15 tracks · 1h 12m 47s (sum of displayed track times)

## 03  Motion

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of rhythm.

**Listening question** — How does the pulse help this music sing?

**Direction** — Hear dance meter as a living structure, not a metronomic constraint. Distinguish pulse, accent, upbeat, and longer rhythmic gesture.

**Recording itinerary** — Perahia: complete French Suites and English Suites; Suzuki/BCJ: complete Orchestral Suite No.2. Focus French Suite No.5 Sarabande and English Suite No.6 Prelude/Sarabande.

**At the piano** — Keep a quiet pulse while singing the phrase; describe where the melodic accent agrees with, or leans against, the meter.

**Playlist tracks** — 36–124 · 89 tracks · 4h 01m 26s (sum of displayed track times)

## 04  Line

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of counterpoint.

**Listening question** — Can I hear each voice as a living line while still hearing why the voices belong together?

**Direction** — Follow independent voices without losing their shared harmonic direction. Every voice must sing, but not every voice must dominate.

**Recording itinerary** — Yo-Yo Ma: six cello suites; Ehnes: violin sonatas and partitas; BWV140/IV→Perahia BWV645; Schiff: 15 Inventions and 15 Sinfonias; Hewitt: Four Duets; Koopman: BWV527.

**At the piano** — Choose two voices; sing one while playing the other, then exchange roles. Mark imitation, arrival and overlap.

**Playlist tracks** — 125–231 · 107 tracks · 5h 54m 21s (sum of displayed track times)

## 05  Rhetoric

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical speech.

**Listening question** — What is this musical gesture saying—and what makes it necessary?

**Direction** — Hear a figure as a meaningful utterance: question, interruption, contrast, elaboration, response.

**Recording itinerary** — Hewitt: BWV992; seven toccatas in curated order 916, 914, 912, 913, 915, 910, 911; BWV903, BWV904 and BWV831; Koopman: BWV542.

**At the piano** — Choose a contrasting passage. Name its rhetorical turn and explain which harmonic or contrapuntal event requires it.

**Playlist tracks** — 232–256 · 25 tracks · 2h 09m 39s (sum of displayed track times)

## 06  Exchange

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical dialogue.

**Listening question** — How does one voice change what I hear in another?

**Direction** — Track entrances, imitation, exchange of roles, solo/tutti contrast and Bach’s reworkings across instruments.

**Recording itinerary** — BWV78 duet; BWV578; flute sonatas BWV1034/1035 and trio BWV1039; gamba sonatas BWV1027–1029 (Hewitt/Müller-Schott); Ehnes concertos; Perahia Italian and keyboard concertos, Brandenburg No.5 and BWV1044; Faust Brandenburg No.4. Pair BWV156/I with BWV1056, BWV1049 with BWV1057, BWV527 with BWV1044.

**At the piano** — Listen twice to a dialogue: once following the initiator, once the responder. Explain how the response changes the first statement.

**Playlist tracks** — 257–315 · 59 tracks · 4h 23m 42s (sum of displayed track times)

## 07  Span

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical form.

**Listening question** — How does what I hear now change the meaning of what I heard before?

**Direction** — Carry musical memory across sections. Hear recurrence as changed by what has intervened.

**Recording itinerary** — Suzuki/BCJ: BWV1068 Air only; Hewitt: BWV989; Suzuki: BWV582; Perahia: all six Partitas BWV825–830. Focus Partitas No.2 and No.4 Allemandes.

**At the piano** — Draw a small map of sections, returns and cadences. Sing the opening after the ending: what has changed in your hearing?

**Playlist tracks** — 316–368 · 53 tracks · 2h 57m 19s (sum of displayed track times)

## 08  Order

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals expressive truth within musical discipline.

**Listening question** — How does Bach make this particular prelude and fugue sing within the discipline of its key, texture and contrapuntal design?

**Direction** — Hear each prelude–fugue pair as a distinct expressive solution to disciplined materials.

**Recording itinerary** — Angela Hewitt’s later Well-Tempered Clavier recording: Books I and II, BWV846–893, all 48 prelude–fugue pairs in order. Landmarks BWV846, 847, 849, 853, 869.

**At the piano** — Identify the fugue subject; sing it through successive entries. Compare its expressive character with its prelude.

**Playlist tracks** — 369–464 · 96 tracks · 4h 30m 20s (sum of displayed track times)

## 09  Transformation

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical identity through change.

**Listening question** — What remains the same when almost everything I hear has changed?

**Direction** — Hear the continuity of the Goldberg Variations beneath changing figuration, genre and character.

**Recording itinerary** — Murray Perahia: BWV988, opening Aria, 30 variations, Aria da capo. Canons every third variation from unison through ninth; Variation 30 is a quodlibet. Focus Variations 13, 25, 30 and the returning Aria.

**At the piano** — After several variations, sing the Aria bass or trace its harmonic framework. Listen to the final Aria without playing along.

**Playlist tracks** — 465–496 · 32 tracks · 1h 13m 10s (sum of displayed track times)

## 10  Convergence

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical relationships in human utterance.

**Listening question** — How do all the relationships I have learned to hear become one meaningful human utterance?

**Direction** — Follow words, voices, instruments and large-scale drama without reducing the music to a single emotion. Attend to the sacred text as part of the work’s meaning.

**Recording itinerary** — Herreweghe/Collegium Vocale Gent: complete St John and St Matthew Passions; Suzuki/BCJ: complete Mass in B minor. Recall BWV12 chorus in the Mass Crucifixus and the bassless BWV105 aria when hearing St Matthew’s Aus Liebe.

**At the piano** — Read a translation alongside the score. Trace one recurring musical relationship and describe how its expressive function changes.

**Playlist tracks** — 497–631 · 135 tracks · 6h 15m 43s (sum of displayed track times)

## 11  Essence

**Principle** — Cantabile reveals the truth of musical relationships themselves.

**Listening question** — Can I hear the music sing when nothing is trying to sound conventionally beautiful?

**Direction** — Hear relation, inversion, canonic procedure and contrapuntal necessity as expressive in themselves.

**Recording itinerary** — Suzuki: complete Musical Offering BWV1079 in recorded order; Hewitt: Art of Fugue BWV1080, including the unfinished final fugue and BWV668a as the recording’s closing chorale. Do not add a second chorale.

**At the piano** — Sing a subject across transformations. At the unfinished fugue, attend to what is present, rather than imagining a compulsory completion.

**Playlist tracks** — 632–668 · 37 tracks · 2h 22m 26s (sum of displayed track times)

# Listening beyond the sequence

The stages are cumulative rather than exclusive. In a fugue, hear Ground, Breath, Motion, Line, Rhetoric, Exchange and Span at once. In a Passion, hear the words as inseparable from harmonic and contrapuntal causes. In the final contrapuntal works, let the relationships themselves sing. Return to the opening Minuet after the final chorale; the first question should sound different.
